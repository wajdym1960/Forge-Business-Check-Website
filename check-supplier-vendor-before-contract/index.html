How to check a supplier or vendor before signing a contract

Before sending money, signing an agreement, or relying on a new supplier, confirm the company’s identity, review relevant public evidence, and record what still needs checking.

Important scope limitThis is a preliminary public-data screen using selected SEC company records and OFAC sanctions data. It is not legal advice, a credit decision, compliance certification, comprehensive due diligence, full KYB, or a guarantee that a supplier is safe.

When a preliminary supplier check is useful

A bounded check can help when your business is considering an unfamiliar supplier, vendor, contractor, distributor, or service provider and needs a clearer basis for the next step. It is most useful before an irreversible commitment—such as sending a deposit, signing a contract, extending terms, or sharing operational access.

A practical five-step check

  1. Confirm the exact company identity

    Ask for the legal name, website, jurisdiction, known trading names, and reliable registration or filing identifiers. A brand name alone may not identify the legal entity receiving your money or signing the agreement.

  2. Check relevant SEC company records

    Use the SEC’s official EDGAR search for the company name, ticker, or CIK when relevant. Confirm that filings refer to the intended entity and review dates and filing context. A private company may legitimately have no SEC filing, so absence is non-adverse.

  3. Search selected OFAC sanctions data

    Use OFAC’s official Sanctions List Search tool for the legal name and known aliases. A similar name is not automatically the same entity; compare locations and available identifiers before drawing a conclusion.

  4. Resolve conflicts and evidence gaps

    Compare names, addresses, jurisdictions, identifiers, and dates across the records you reviewed. Record ambiguous or missing evidence instead of treating it as proof that the company is trustworthy.

  5. Match the next step to the decision

    Use the evidence to decide whether to proceed with the preliminary relationship, pause for more information, or obtain appropriate specialist review. Higher-value or higher-risk commitments may require sources beyond this bounded check.

Red flags that need more review

  • The legal entity name does not match the website, invoice, or contract.
  • Addresses, jurisdictions, or identifiers conflict across the available evidence.
  • An OFAC search produces a plausible name match that cannot be resolved from identifiers and context.
  • The supplier will not provide enough information to identify the entity receiving payment.
  • The available evidence is too limited to support a confident preliminary decision.

What this check does not cover

Selected SEC and OFAC data does not establish beneficial ownership, licensing, financial condition, creditworthiness, litigation history, product quality, contract enforceability, or compliance with every relevant jurisdiction. No match in one search is not proof that a supplier is safe.
