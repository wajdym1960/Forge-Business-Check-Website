OFAC company-name screening guide

How to check whether a company appears on an OFAC sanctions list

Start with the company’s precise identity, search official OFAC data, and review any possible match before drawing a conclusion.

Important limitationA name search is an initial evidence step. A possible match does not by itself prove that the intended company is sanctioned, and no match does not prove that a company is safe, legitimate, or legally compliant.

A careful four-step OFAC company check

  1. Identify the intended company

    Collect the legal name, known aliases, jurisdiction, address, website, and reliable registration or filing identifiers. A common or translated name can refer to more than one organization.

  2. Search official OFAC data

    Use the OFAC Sanctions List Search tool and review the current Sanctions List Service. Search the legal name and relevant aliases rather than relying on a third-party snippet.

  3. Review a possible match

    Compare the returned name, aliases, addresses, programs, entity type, and available identifiers with the company you intend to check. OFAC describes its search as using fuzzy logic, so similarity alone is not confirmation.

  4. Record the evidence and uncertainty

    Document the search date, names used, records reviewed, and unresolved differences. If identifiers do not support a responsible conclusion, preserve the result as unresolved or insufficient evidence.

What a possible OFAC match means

It means the searched name produced a result that deserves contextual review. It does not automatically establish that the result and the intended company are the same entity.

  • Check aliases and spelling variations.
  • Compare addresses, jurisdictions, and entity types.
  • Review available identification numbers and program details.
  • Escalate ambiguity instead of converting similarity into certainty.

What a no-match result cannot prove

  • That the company is safe, trustworthy, financially sound, or legally compliant.
  • That every alias, owner, affiliate, or related party was searched.
  • That other sanctions lists, ownership rules, licenses, or jurisdiction-specific records are clear.
  • That comprehensive KYB or due diligence has been completed.

Why human review matters

Names, aliases, transliterations, addresses, ownership information, and source updates can make sanctions screening ambiguous. Human review helps determine whether evidence refers to the intended company and whether the available identifiers are sufficient.

Frequently asked questions

Does an OFAC name match prove the company is sanctioned?

No. A possible name match requires identity and contextual review before it can be connected to the intended company.

Does no OFAC name match prove the company is safe?

No. It describes only the searched data, names, and inputs. It is not a safety guarantee, legal conclusion, or complete due-diligence result.

Should I also review SEC company records?

SEC records can add selected identity and filing evidence for covered filers, but they do not cover every company. See the SEC and OFAC company-check guide.
